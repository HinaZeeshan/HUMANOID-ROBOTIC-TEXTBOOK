  # Chapter 6: Humanoid Kinematics
     
     ## 6.1 Introduction to Kinematics
     
     Kinematics is the study of motion without considering the forces that cause it. In robotics, it primarily deals with th      spatial configuration of the robot, such as the position and orientation of its end-effector relative to its base.     
     
     ## 6.2 Forward Kinematics
     
     Forward kinematics is the process of calculating the position and orientation of the end-effector given the joint angle      of the robot.
    
    ## 6.3 Inverse Kinematics
    
    Inverse kinematics is the more challenging problem of calculating the joint angles required to achieve a desired positi      and orientation of the end-effector. This is crucial for controlling a robot's movements to reach specific points in   
      space.
    
    ## 6.4 Kinematic Chains and Degrees of Freedom
    
    ### Serial Kinematic Chains
    
    Robots are often modeled as a series of rigid links connected by joints, forming a kinematic chain.
    
    ### Degrees of Freedom (DoF)
    
    The number of independent parameters that define the configuration of a mechanical system. Humanoids typically have man      DoF, making IK complex.
